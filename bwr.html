    Recorder

    Automatic control iThe MG set speed, the feed pump speed, the feed valve, the feed pump's minimum-flow valve, the two heater drain valves, the storage tank's makeup and reject valves, the cleanup reject, the turbine control valve and the bypass valve each have an M/A switch. In A the slider follows the automatic demand and is greyed out. In M the slider is yours and a notch marks the demand: your value plus the correction the controller would make for the present pressure or level error. It sits on your value when the error is zero, and switching to A starts from the notch. The control rods, the recirculation pump breaker, the two extraction valves, the pump run switches, the isolation and stop valves, the safety/relief valves and the condenser (circulating water pump, valve and inlet temperature, air ejector, vacuum pump, vacuum breaker) are always manual: BWRs have no automatic rod control (power is changed with the rods and with the core flow), and the rest are set once for the operating state. The safety/relief valves' springs open them on pressure, and the reactor protection scrams, isolates and trips whatever you do. Nothing switches between M and A by itself.

    Core flow (MG set speed) iProportional-integral on the core flow (100 % = 12,900 kg/s): 10 % of rated flow low raises the MG set speed demand 3 %, integral 5 s. The MG set's speed limiter lets the speed change at most 2 % per second and holds it at 20 % or more while the pump runs. Power follows the flow along the rod line through the void feedback: at 40 % flow it settles near 65 %.

    Proportional-integral on core flow, acting on the MG set speed.

    Pressure regulator (control and bypass valves) iAs on the GE electro-hydraulic control, simplified: a proportional-integral term on dome pressure gives a steam flow demand (0.1 MPa above the setpoint asks for 34 % of rated steam more, integral 30 s). The turbine control valve gets the demand up to the load limit; the bypass valve gets what the turbine is not taking: the excess over the load limit, or everything once the stop valve shuts. Each demand becomes a valve position through the valve's characteristic at the rated throttle pressure. Below the setpoint both valves close, so with little heat the pressure rises to the setpoint and then holds there. A real regulator senses the throttle pressure at the turbine; this one senses the dome.

    Proportional-integral on dome pressure: the control valve takes steam up to the load limit, the bypass the rest.

    Feedwater level control (feed valve) iThree-element: the feed flow demand follows the main steam flow, plus a proportional-integral term on the level error (800 kg/s per metre, integral 60 s). A flow controller then moves the feed valve until the measured feed flow matches the demand (proportional-integral, integral 4 s). Steam through the safety/relief valves is not in the steam-flow signal, so the level term has to make up for it. The valve can only pass what the pump delivers: if the vessel pressure rises past the pump's shutoff head, no valve opening helps.

    Three-element: steam flow plus a level correction sets the feed demand; a flow loop moves the valve.

    Feed pump speed iProportional-integral on the pump discharge pressure minus dome pressure, as turbine-driven feed pumps are run: the speed follows the vessel pressure, so the feed valve always has about the same pressure drop to work with (2.2 MPa at rated, 0.3 MPa of it in the heater and piping). 0.1 MPa of error moves the speed 0.15 %, integral 10 s. The speed changes at most 10 % per second.

    Holds the pump discharge a set margin above dome pressure.

    Feed pump minimum flow iThe recirculation valve from the feed pump discharge back to the condenser opens in proportion as the flow to the vessel falls below 25 % of rated (447 kg/s), fully at no forward flow, so the pump never runs dead-headed. Proportional only.

    Opens the recirculation valve below 25 % of rated feed flow.

    Heater drain levels iEach heater shell's level is held half full by its own proportional-integral controller: the HP heater on its drain valve (the drains cascade to the LP heater), the LP heater and its drain tank on the drain pump's discharge valve. A quarter of the shell above the setpoint strokes the valve fully open; integral 30 s.

    Each heater shell is held half full by its drain valve.

    Hotwell level (makeup and reject) iOne controller holds the hotwell half full, split range over two valves: its output, a net makeup in kg/s, opens the makeup valve (storage tank to condenser) when positive and the reject valve (condensate pump discharge back to the tank) when negative, 1 % of stroke per kg/s, so the two are never both asked open. The output is what the condensate pump sends on (feed plus minimum-flow recirculation, less the drains pumped forward) minus the condensate return, plus a proportional-integral term on the hotwell level (10 % off asks for the full stroke, integral 300 s). Each valve has its own M/A switch; with one in M the other still follows the controller.

    Holds the hotwell half full: makeup when low, reject when high.

    Things to try

    1. Startup. The vessel is at 100 °C and atmospheric pressure with the head vent just shut, the level at 14.0 m, the rods all in and the reactor subcritical, the recirculation pump at its 20 % minimum speed, the mode switch in Startup. The MSIVs are open, the turbine stop valve shut; the bypass valve in A will hold 7 MPa once the pressure gets there. The condenser starts open to atmosphere, full of air, with the vacuum pump running: it pulls the shell down to about 19 kPa in an hour and 12 kPa in two. The turbine is tripped on low vacuum until you reset it with a vacuum pulled. Withdraw the rods: the reactor goes critical near 60 % withdrawn, and the period readout shows how fast the power rises; hold it to a period of a minute or more. Heat-up then follows the power: keep it under 55 °C per hour by trimming the rods. The water expands about 3 m on the way to 7 MPa; the cleanup reject in A sends up to 10 kg/s of it to the condenser while the feed valve stays shut. Heat up much faster than 55 °C/h and the expansion outruns the reject near rated pressure: the level reaches L8, which trips the feed pump and the turbine. Once steam flows, press Air ejector steam and stop the vacuum pump when the ejector has the condenser below about 15 kPa. Switch the mode to Run before the power passes 15 %, where the Startup APRM trip scrams it.
    2. Rated power. Every control starts in A, balanced: rods 80 % withdrawn, core flow 100 %, feed pump at full speed, feed valve 75 % open, feed at 216 °C. Change the power the BWR way, with the core flow setpoint: at 75 % flow the voids grow, the void feedback takes the power down to about 87 % and the steam, feed and extraction flows follow; back at 100 % it returns. Two percent of rod insertion takes about 8 % off the power. Trip the recirculation pump: the pump coasts down, natural circulation keeps about a third of the flow and the power settles near 58 %.
    3. Turbine trip at rated power, everything in A. The stop valve shuts in 0.2 s and its position switch scrams the reactor; the bypass valve opens fully but passes only 25 % of rated steam, and the pressure peaks near 7.4 MPa, just over the 7.31 MPa high-pressure scram setting, below the safety/relief valves. The voids collapse and the level drops through L3 (a second scram signal) and recovers; the feedwater controller sets its level setpoint down 0.45 m after a scram, so the refill does not overshoot to L8. The bypass then carries the decay heat to the condenser at 7 MPa. Set the load limit below 100 % at rated power instead, and the bypass takes the steam the turbine may not.
    4. Close MSIVs at rated power. Both valves stroke shut over 4 s; below 90 % open their position switches scram the reactor, and the low header pressure that follows isolates them in the protection logic too. Pressure climbs past the high-pressure setting and the four safety/relief valves set lowest (7.62 MPa) lift on their springs and cycle as in a real isolation, each lift blowing steam to the suppression pool, which warms. Nothing reaches the turbine or the condenser, so the feed cools to the hotwell's temperature; the feed valve in A keeps the level a little below normal (the setdown), the swell of each relief valve lift showing on it.
    5. Isolation at 6 % (200 MW, critical with the rods at the matching position). The MSIVs start closing; the pressure reaches the high-pressure scram setting in about 30 s and the reactor scrams before the safety/relief valves lift. The feed valve in A keeps the level, and the makeup valve in A tops up the hotwell from the storage tank for the steam that went to the pool instead of back to it; switch it to M, shut, and watch the hotwell drain. Open one or two valves by hand to depressurise steadily, as for a cooldown.
    6. Hot standby. Critical at 1.5 % of rated power with the stop valve shut and the bypass in A passing about 20 kg/s. With the rods fixed the feed flow and the power swing together over a few minutes: cold feed collapses voids, the void feedback raises the power, the level controller answers. A real plant runs its startup level controller and a small feed valve here.
    7. Lose feedwater heating at rated power with everything in A. Both extraction valves throttle to 20 %, the heater shell pressures drop and the feed reaches the vessel colder within a minute. The colder water at the core inlet collapses voids, and the void feedback raises the power: the APRM trips at 120 % after about 50 s and the reactor scrams. A real core gains about 10 to 15 % on the loss of a heater string and stays in; this core's power responds to the inlet temperature about twice as strongly (flat axial power, one core node).
    8. HP drain valve fails shut at rated power. The HP heater's drains have nowhere to go: its shell fills in about 20 s, the water covers more and more of the tubes, and over 80 % the shell's high-level dump spills to the condenser. With most of its tubes flooded the heater heats the feed far less, and, as with the loss of feedwater heating, the power rises until the APRM scrams it after about a minute. Press the event again to repair the valve; its level controller drains the shell back to half full.
    9. Stop the drain pump at rated power. The LP heater drains and the cascaded HP drains collect in the drain tank, which fills, floods the LP heater's tubes and dumps to the condenser. The condensate pump takes the whole feed flow, the condenser takes the drains' heat, the feed arrives 4 K colder and the power rises about 2 %, while the stub's output falls from about 1117 to 1020 MWe.
    10. Stop the condensate pump at rated power. The feed pump loses its suction pressure: within a second it is below the 0.7 MPa trip setting, the pump cavitates and delivers almost nothing, and after 5 s it trips. The level falls through L3 (scram) about 15 s after the pump stops and L2 (MSIV isolation) about 15 s after that. Start the condensate pump again: the feed pump stays tripped until you switch it to stop and back to run, and its speed then comes back at 10 % per second.
    11. Lose the condenser at rated power. Stop the CW pump: the circulating water coasts down over about 12 s, the steam stops condensing and the vacuum goes. Within about 20 s the vacuum falls past 23 inHg, the low-vacuum scram, and 22.5 inHg, where the turbine trips; at 7 inHg the MSIVs isolate and the bypass valves are blocked, so the steam goes to the safety/relief valves. Gentler: raise the circulating water inlet to 30 °C, throttle its valve, or shut the air ejector steam: air builds up, blankets the tubes, and the back pressure climbs over an hour or so.
    12. Protection. Each trip that fires lights its tile and is logged with the time. Reset trips clears those whose signal has cleared; the rest stay in. After any scram the rod lever is at 0, so a reset does not pull the rods again. A tripped feed pump (L8) stays off until its trip is reset: there is no RCIC or HPCI, so after an isolation with the feed pump tripped the level boils down until you reset it. The mode switch in Shutdown scrams the reactor at once.
    13. Storage tank. During an isolation the makeup valve in A replaces from the tank the steam the safety/relief valves send to the pool, and the tank level falls. Put the reject valve in M and open it at rated: the makeup valve in A opens to match, and the two circulate condensate through the tank, warming it.
    14. Depressurise. At hot standby switch the bypass valve to M and open it wide: it passes about 450 kg/s. The water flashes as the pressure falls, the voids grow and the level rises at first, before the lost inventory brings it down. In Run the main steam line low-pressure isolation would close the MSIVs at 850 psig; in Startup it is bypassed.

    What the model solves

    The vessel (600 m³ of free volume, 21 m tall) holds water and steam at one pressure. It tracks the total mass and energy, and the steam mass. Each step it finds the pressure at which the water and the saturated steam exactly fill the vessel. Water hotter than saturation at that pressure flashes at once, so a falling pressure makes steam throughout the water. Water below saturation, after cold feedwater or cooling, condenses steam at a finite rate (10 s time constant), so pressure drops after a cold-water injection.

    The steam the water makes passes through it as bubbles before it reaches the dome. These voids take up volume inside the two-phase mixture, so they raise the level without changing the pressure. They clear with a 0.7 s time constant, sized to give about 35 m³ of voids, about 1.2 m of swell, at rated power. The level shown is the height of water plus voids over the vessel's mean cross-section; the recorder also shows the collapsed level, water alone.

    The core is one point-kinetics node: six delayed-neutron groups, a lumped fuel temperature (5 s time constant) and seven decay-heat groups. Reactivity comes from the rod lever and three feedbacks: core voids (−0.1 % per % void), fuel temperature (Doppler) and core inlet temperature. The core voids follow from the core flow, the inlet subcooling and the power (drift flux, flat axial power), so more flow or colder inlet water means fewer voids and more power. A scram drives the rods in over about 3 s; the fission power falls in seconds and the decay heat stays (about 5 % at 10 s, 3 % at a minute).

    The recirculation pump drives the jet pumps through an MG set whose speed changes at most 2 % a second, between 20 % and full. The core flow is where the jet pump head and the natural circulation head (the chimney's voids against the downcomer) balance the losses, with the loop's inertia; with the pump tripped natural circulation keeps about a third of rated flow. Feed mixes into the downcomer with the water from the separators, so the core inlet is subcooled.

    Reactor protection is part of the plant: each trip compares one signal with its typical BWR/4 setpoint, latches when it fires and is logged. Scram on high pressure (1045 psig), low level (L3), APRM high flux (120 % in Run, 15 % in Startup), MSIV closure (Run), turbine stop valve closure above 30 % turbine flow, low condenser vacuum (Run) and the mode switch in Shutdown; MSIV isolation on low-low level (L2), low steam line pressure (Run), high steam flow and very low vacuum (Run); turbine trip on high level (L8) and low vacuum; feed pump trip on L8. The bypass valves are blocked while the vacuum is below 7 inHg.

    The core's heat goes into the water, with the vessel's own heat losses taken out through the steel. Steam leaves through the main steam line and the safety/relief valves at saturated-steam enthalpy; feedwater enters as compressed liquid at the heater outlet temperature.

    The condensate and feed train: the condensate pump draws from the hotwell and pushes the water through the polisher and the LP heater to the feed pump, which raises it to the vessel through the HP heater and the feed valve; a minimum-flow line takes water from the feed pump discharge back to the condenser. The pipes hold no water, so each step the model solves the whole train at once: the pressures between the pumps are where every pump passes what the lines after it take. The condensate pump (constant speed, 2.6 MPa at shutoff) and the feed pump (variable speed, 7.4 MPa more at full speed) develop at most 10 MPa together, so the same feed valve opening passes more water when the vessel pressure falls. The condensate pump needs net positive suction head: the hotwell level plus a 1 m pit over the vapour pressure, 3 m at half full against 2 m needed at rated flow, so as the hotwell runs low it cavitates and loses head.

    Heater drains: each heater shell holds the water its steam condenses to (30 m³ HP, 60 m³ LP with its drain tank), saturated at the shell pressure. The HP drains cascade through the HP drain valve to the LP heater, where they flash and do over half its heating; the LP drains and the cascaded HP drains collect in the drain tank, and the drain pump forwards them into the condensate line after the LP heater, so at rated about 580 kg/s of hot water bypasses the condensate pump and the condenser. Above 60 % full the water covers tubes and the heater heats less; above 80 % a dump valve spills to the condenser.

    Two heaters, each a shell in which steam extracted from the turbine condenses on the feed tubes. Each extraction pressure rises in proportion to the main steam flow: 0.28 MPa for the LP heater, 2.4 MPa for the HP heater at rated. Each extraction valve's opening sets how much steam reaches its shell, and so the shell pressure; the feed leaves 3 K below the shell's saturation temperature, 126 °C from the LP heater and 216 °C from the HP heater at rated. With no steam to the turbine there is no heating, and the feed comes in at the hotwell temperature.

    The condenser holds steam and air. Each step it finds the shell temperature at which the steam that arrives, less what the tubes condense, fills the shell saturated; the air adds its own partial pressure on top. The air mass changes with the in-leakage and what the air ejector or vacuum pump take out, and the more air there is at the tubes the less they condense. The circulating water flow follows its pump and valve, and its inlet temperature is your setting.

    Assumptions and limits

    • Core: one point-kinetics node with generic BWR/4 feedback coefficients, not fitted to a plant: no flux shape, xenon, burnup or local peaking, flat axial power. The power responds to the core inlet temperature about twice as strongly as a real core, so a loss of feedwater heating scrams on APRM here. One rod lever stands for the rod pattern (critical near 60 % withdrawn hot, about 80 % at rated); the scram is a 3 s stroke placeholder for the control rod drives. The model stops if the reactor reaches prompt criticality.
    • Recirculation: one loop stands for two, one pump on one MG set, one jet pump stands for twenty. No pump heat, no flow-control valve, no end-of-cycle pump trip. The downcomer is one mixed region; no stratification or steam carry-under.
    • Protection: one logic channel, no voting, no instrument delays or errors (the signals are the model's own values). No high drywell pressure trip (no drywell), and nothing starts on L2 or L1: no RCIC, HPCI, core spray or automatic depressurisation. After a scram the feedwater controller sets its level setpoint down 0.45 m for 10 minutes and ramps it back over 20 (illustrative).
    • The steam is always saturated, so there is no superheat in the dome. The dome holds no air or nitrogen: below 100 °C the pressure would fall below atmospheric, where a real vessel would have its head vent open.
    • Level is the volume of water and voids over a uniform cross-section of 28.6 m², from the inside bottom of the vessel: top of active fuel 9.3 m, normal level 14.3 m, main steam nozzle 16.5 m. Real vessels measure level in the downcomer, against a reference leg. The model stops if the level falls to the top of the fuel or reaches the steam nozzle.
    • Main steam line: one line with an inboard and an outboard isolation valve (MSIV), then a header that feeds the turbine through the stop valve and the control valve, and the condenser through the bypass valve. The pipes hold no steam: the header pressure is where what the line and the MSIVs pass (0.3 MPa below the dome at rated flow) equals what the valves beyond pass (choked, in proportion to header pressure). Turbine valves wide open pass 105 % of rated steam; the control valve is about 86 % open at rated, with an illustrative characteristic that flattens near full open. The bypass passes 25 % of rated, straight to the condenser, where it arrives superheated. MSIVs stroke in 4 s, the stop valve trips shut in 0.2 s and opens in 5 s, the control valve strokes in 5 s and the bypass in 0.5 s; MSIV flow area is in proportion to its position, so most of the flow is cut in the last part of the stroke. Line drop, valve sizes and stroke times other than the MSIV closure are illustrative, not from one plant. The protection trips the turbine and closes the MSIVs whatever their switches say.
    • Turbine stub: there is no turbine model yet. The steam expands along one fixed line from the throttle to the condenser pressure with an isentropic efficiency of 0.85, the heater's extraction steam leaving on the way, and the gross output is that work times 0.985. No moisture separator reheater, no shaft speed or stage pressures. With two heater stages (about 417 kg/s at 2.4 MPa and 163 kg/s at 0.28 MPa; the HP drains flashing in the LP heater do the rest of its heating) and the drains pumped forward, the output is about 1117 MWe at rated, close to a real plant's 1100 MWe, which has five or six stages but loses more to moisture and reheat.
    • Condenser: one steam space of 4000 m³ over the hotwell, saturated at its own temperature. The exhaust and the heater drains flash or condense into it, and the tube bundle condenses steam at a rate set by the circulating water: 38 m³/s at 20 °C with the pump on and its valve open, through a bundle sized (not sourced) for about 7 kPa at rated. The condensate falls into the hotwell at the shell temperature, so the hotwell and the condensate pump suction follow the load: 38 °C at rated, colder at low load. Above 0.135 MPa the LP turbine exhaust diaphragms would blow, and the model stops.
    • Safety/relief valves: eleven, each with a spring set pressure and a rated capacity from a GE BWR/4 training lesson plan: four at 1090 psig (7.62 MPa abs) rated 800,000 lb/h (101 kg/s), four at 1100 psig (7.69 MPa) 808,000 lb/h, three at 1110 psig (7.75 MPa) 815,000 lb/h. Hover a valve's button for its opening pressure and capacity. A spring opens its valve fully above the set pressure and recloses it 0.25 MPa lower; that blowdown is an illustrative figure, not from the source. Flow scales in proportion to pressure from the rated capacity, into the suppression pool at 0.1 MPa. Opening by hand stands for the valve's relief (pneumatic) mode; there is no automatic relief logic, no low-low set and no automatic depressurisation system.
    • Feedwater: the hotwell is the water under the condenser; it changes with the condensate, makeup, feed, and steam lost through the safety/relief valves. It holds 600 t. Condensate storage tank: 1900 t (about 500,000 US gal), 12 m tall, open to the air, starts 70 % full at 30 °C; one mass and temperature, no heat loss to the air, overflow stops the model. The makeup valve passes about 100 kg/s fully open from a 70 % full tank into the condenser vacuum (the tank's head plus the vacuum drive it; less with the vacuum lost or the tank low, none below 2 %); the reject valve passes about 100 kg/s fully open from the condensate pump discharge. Valve sizes and elevations are illustrative; no demineralised-water fill of the tank, no other CST users (core spray, HPCI and RCIC suction are not modelled). One condensate pump (standing for the condensate and booster pumps; 2.6 MPa shutoff, constant speed) and one turbine-driven feed pump (variable speed, 7.4 MPa more at full speed; its drive steam is not modelled), with illustrative curves; real plants have three of each. The condensate pump loses head as its NPSH runs out (required 2 m at rated flow, rising with the square of the flow; available from the hotwell level, a 1 m pit and the hotwell's subcooling), and below 2 % hotwell its suction uncovers. The feed pump trips on suction pressure below 0.7 MPa for 5 s (illustrative setting), and cavitates if its suction would fall below the vapour pressure. The polisher is a pressure drop only. Minimum-flow valve sized for 25 % of rated flow; no startup low-flow valve (the feed valve throttles down to zero). Two heater stages (LP and HP) stand for the train, each with one throttling valve on its extraction line; real plants have five or six stages whose extraction lines carry only isolation and non-return valves. The feed pump's own heating of the water is ignored. The outlet follows with a 60 s lag. Heater drains: shell volumes, the 60 % tube-flooding level (heating falls in proportion to the flooded tubes, a crude stand-in for lost condensing area), the 80 % dump, the drain valve sizes and the drain pump curve (3.5 MPa shutoff; no NPSH model, it loses head only as the drain tank empties) are illustrative. Each shell is saturated at its pressure, which follows the steam flow at once (no shell steam volume or heat capacity); the LP shell vents to the condenser any flash steam its tubes can't condense. No drain coolers, no separate drain tank level, no MSR drains. Reactor water cleanup is a placeholder: a reject of up to 10 kg/s of vessel water to the condenser (in A it opens as the level rises 0.1 to 0.4 m above the setpoint), so the swell of a heat-up has somewhere to go. No cleanup heat exchangers, pumps or filters.
    • Circulating water: an in/out pipe. One pump (on or off) against a fixed head and system resistance, with a butterfly valve; the open valve passes 38 m³/s. The flow follows the pump and valve with a 12 s lag. No pump curve, no river or cooling tower: the inlet temperature is whatever you set. Illustrative numbers.
    • Condenser air: the shell holds air as well as steam, at one temperature (Dalton): the pressure is the steam's saturation pressure plus the air's. Air leaks in at about 15 scfm (0.009 kg/s) while the shell is below atmospheric, and radiolytic hydrogen and oxygen from the core arrive with the main steam (0.02 kg/s at rated, counted as air, not taken out of the vessel water). Air near the tubes blankets them: the heat transfer falls as 1/(1 + (air fraction/0.1)²), and the clean bundle is sized so the rated point (about 260 Pa of air; 7.3 kPa with one heater stage, 6.8 kPa with two) matches the bundle without air. The air fraction is a shell average; a real condenser gathers the air in an air-cooling section. The steam jet air ejector removes an effective 10 m³/s of shell gas, needs main steam above 0.7 MPa (full capacity at 1.2 MPa) and breaks when its suction rises above 25 to 35 kPa; its 2.5 kg/s of motive steam is taken off the main steam ahead of the turbine, so with the MSIVs shut it has none. The mechanical vacuum pump moves 2.2 m³/s, falling to nothing at 7 kPa. The vacuum breaker admits air from atmosphere. No off-gas train: the removed air leaves the model. All illustrative numbers.
    • Plant layout: the mimic draws the whole plant as a draft. Solid parts are modelled; dashed grey parts (the containment outline and the moisture separator reheater) are drawn only. The HP heater's extraction is drawn from the HP turbine and the LP heater's from the LP turbine. The heater drain lines (HP drain valve down to the LP heater, drain tank, drain pump and its valve into the condensate line) were not on the original layout; they are drawn in water blue while they flow. The heaters' high-level dumps to the condenser are not drawn; their flows show beside each heater. The HP and LP turbines and the generator are drawn solid for the turbine stub; the recirculation pump, its loop and the jet pump for the recirculation model.
    • Vessel and internals steel: one node of 6×10⁸ J/K (about 1200 t), exchanging 1 MW/K with the water and losing about 1 MW through the insulation to a drywell at a fixed 50 °C. It lags the water by about 10 K during a 55 °C/h heat-up and slows heat-up and cooldown. Illustrative numbers. Generic numbers for a large BWR (about 3300 MW thermal); not any specific plant. All pressures are absolute.
    • Level instruments: the level is also shown in inches above instrument zero (528 in above the vessel bottom), as BWR operators read it, with the usual BWR/4 setpoints marked: L8 +54 in, L3 +12.5 in, L2 −38 in, L1 −129 in. Typical values; plants differ. The instrument reads the model's level directly (no reference-leg errors).
    • Suppression pool: 3800 t of water at 32 °C; SRV steam condenses completely in it, so it warms about 1 K for every 6 t of steam. Pool cooling is a placeholder for an RHR loop (deferred): a fixed cooler, 1.5 MW per kelvin above 30 °C service water. Its limits are shown: 35 °C normal, 43.3 °C scram, 48.9 °C depressurise. No containment pressure: at 100 °C the model stops.
