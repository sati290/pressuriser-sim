BWR reactor vessel simulator

A boiling-water reactor pressure vessel with its main steam line, safety/relief valves, a turbine stub, the condenser and the feedwater train: hotwell, feed pump, heater and feed valve. The core has point kinetics with void, Doppler and moderator feedback, one control rod lever, and a recirculation pump on an MG set driving a jet pump. Start up from 100 °C, run it at power, change the core flow, isolate it, and watch power, pressure, level and the core voids interact. PWR pressuriser simulator

Automatic control

The MG set speed, the feed pump speed, the feed valve, the hotwell makeup and the steam valve each have an M/A switch. In A the slider follows the automatic demand and is greyed out. In M the slider is yours and a notch marks the demand: your value plus the correction the controller would make for the present pressure or level error. It sits on your value when the error is zero, and switching to A starts from the notch. The control rods, the recirculation pump breaker, the extraction valve and the safety/relief valves are always manual (BWRs have no automatic rod control: power is changed with the rods and with the core flow); the valves' springs open them on pressure whatever you do. Nothing switches between M and A by itself.

Core flow (MG set speed)

Proportional-integral on the core flow (100 % = 12,900 kg/s): 10 % of rated flow low raises the MG set speed demand 3 %, integral 5 s. The MG set's speed limiter lets the speed change at most 2 % per second and holds it at 20 % or more while the pump runs. Power follows the flow along the rod line through the void feedback: at 40 % flow it settles near 65 %.

Pressure regulator (steam valve)

Proportional-integral on dome pressure: 0.1 MPa above the setpoint opens the valve a further 30 %, and the integral (30 s) removes the offset. Below the setpoint it closes the valve, so with little heat the pressure rises to the setpoint and then holds there.

Feedwater level control (feed valve)

Three-element: the feed flow demand follows the main steam flow, plus a proportional-integral term on the level error (800 kg/s per metre, integral 60 s). A flow controller then moves the feed valve until the measured feed flow matches the demand (proportional-integral, integral 4 s). Steam through the safety/relief valves is not in the steam-flow signal, so the level term has to make up for it. The valve can only pass what the pump delivers: if the vessel pressure rises past the pump's shutoff head, no valve opening helps.

Feed pump speed

Proportional-integral on the pump discharge pressure minus dome pressure, as turbine-driven feed pumps are run: the speed follows the vessel pressure, so the feed valve always has about the same pressure drop to work with (2.2 MPa at rated, 0.3 MPa of it in the heater and piping). 0.1 MPa of error moves the speed 0.15 %, integral 10 s. The speed changes at most 10 % per second.

Hotwell level (makeup)

Makeup from the condensate storage tank, or reject to it when negative, holds the hotwell half full: feed flow minus condensate return, plus a proportional-integral term on the hotwell level (10 % low asks for the full 100 kg/s, integral 300 s).

Things to try

  1. Startup. The vessel is at 100 °C and atmospheric pressure with the head vent just shut, the level low at 11.5 m, every valve shut, the feed pump stopped, the recirculation pump at its minimum 20 % speed and the rods fully in: the core is subcritical and sits at the source level, a few watts. Withdraw the rods (the lever, 0.1 % per second at most). As reactivity approaches zero the source multiplication grows and the power climbs by decades; near 58 % the core goes critical and the period becomes positive. At atmospheric pressure the voids are large (steam is 1600 times lighter than water), so the void feedback holds the power to a few tens of MW until the water heats. Keep the heat-up rate near the usual 55 °C/h limit by moving the rods. Every control starts in A except the feed valve and the MG set speed, which start in M; the feed valve stays shut because the water expands about 3 m on the way to 7 MPa and the level controller (which can only add water) would fill the vessel to the steam nozzle. Open it by hand once the steam valve is passing steam; switch it to A once the level has stopped swelling. As the pressure rises the voids shrink and the power rises with them: watch the period. The steam valve in A opens when pressure reaches 7 MPa and holds it there. The pump speed in A keeps the feed pump discharge 2.2 MPa above the vessel; near 7 MPa it needs more than 84 % speed to deliver anything.
  2. Rated power. Rods 80 % withdrawn, core flow 100 %, every control in A, balanced: feed pump at full speed, feed valve 75 % open, heater at 216 °C. Change the power the BWR way, with the core flow: set the core flow setpoint to 40 % and the MG set runs down at 2 % per second, the voids grow, and the power settles near 65 % on the same rod line; back to 100 % and it returns. Or move the rods: 75 % gives about 77 % power. Less power means fewer voids and a lower level (shrink); the feedwater controller sees the level fall and the steam flow drop at the same time. Trip the recirculation pump: it coasts down in a few seconds, the power dips below 40 %, then natural circulation settles at about a third of rated flow and 58 % power.
  3. Close main steam valve at rated power. The valve strokes shut in 5 s, pressure climbs, the core voids collapse (the downcomer water entering the core is further below the new saturation temperature) and the power spikes to about 150 % within 5 s. Nothing scrams automatically yet: all eleven safety/relief valves open on their springs but pass about 1130 kg/s, less than the core makes, so pressure keeps climbing, past the feed pump's 10 MPa shutoff head, and the core uncovers after about 80 s. Press Scram as the valve closes: only the lowest bank of four lifts, pressure peaks just above 7.6 MPa, and they cycle as in a real isolation. With no steam to the turbine there is no extraction steam, so the feed cools to the hotwell's temperature, which falls towards the circulating water's as the condenser unloads and the level controller overshoots by about 1.2 m before it settles.
  4. Isolation at 6 % power. The core is critical at 200 MW (rods set for it, recirculation at 30 % speed, no scram) when the steam valve, in M, starts closing; everything else is in A. As the pressure rises the voids shrink and the power rises to about 330 MW, and the four valves set lowest (7.62 MPa) lift on their springs and cycle, about once every 40 s. Each lift drops the pressure 0.25 MPa and blows inventory to the suppression pool. The feed valve in A keeps the level, and makeup in A tops up the hotwell for the steam that went to the pool instead of back to it; switch makeup to M at zero and watch the hotwell drain. Insert rods or scram to bring the power down, then open one or two valves by hand to depressurise steadily, as for a cooldown.
  5. Lose feedwater heating at rated power with everything in A. The extraction valve throttles to 20 %, the heater shell pressure drops, and the feed reaches the vessel about 35 K colder within a minute, 54 K colder after a few. Colder feedwater reaches the core inlet a few seconds later through the downcomer: the water boils higher up the core, the voids shrink, and the void feedback raises the power, about 18 % after a minute and 40 % after six with the rods and flow unchanged. That is stronger than real plants show for this event (roughly 15–25 %), because the core is one node with flat axial power, which exaggerates how far the boiling boundary moves. Insert rods or lower the core flow to bring it back.
  6. Hot standby. Critical at 1.5 % power (50 MW), rods near 61 %, recirculation at 30 % speed, pressure regulator in A. With no automatic rod control the power drifts as the feed temperature and the hotwell change: hold it with small rod moves.
  7. Depressurise. At hot standby open the steam valve wide by hand. The water flashes as the pressure falls, the voids grow and the level rises at first, before the lost inventory brings it down.

What the model solves

The vessel (600 m³ of free volume, 21 m tall) holds water and steam at one pressure. It tracks the total mass and energy, and the steam mass. Each step it finds the pressure at which the water and the saturated steam exactly fill the vessel. Water hotter than saturation at that pressure flashes at once, so a falling pressure makes steam throughout the water. Water below saturation, after cold feedwater or cooling, condenses steam at a finite rate (10 s time constant), so pressure drops after a cold-water injection.

The water is two regions. The downcomer (100 m³ outside the shroud and in the lower plenum) takes the feedwater and the water returning from the separators and feeds the jet pumps, so it is a few kelvin subcooled at power (9 K at rated) and carries a colder feed to the core inlet with a delay of a few seconds. The core region boils; it may not stay above saturation, and below saturation it condenses steam with a 10 s time constant. A pressure rise leaves the downcomer further below saturation, so the core inlet subcooling rises and the voids shrink.

The core: point kinetics with six delayed-neutron groups (β 0.0056, prompt-jump approximation, a small neutron source), seven decay-heat groups that reproduce the (1 + t)^−0.2 shape after long operation, and one fuel node (5 s time constant, about 615 °C average at rated) that passes the heat to the coolant. Reactivity is the rods plus feedback: void −1×10⁻³ per % of core-average void, Doppler −2×10⁻⁵ per K of fuel temperature, moderator −2×10⁻⁵ per K of core inlet temperature, all relative to hot zero power. The core void comes from the core's thermal-hydraulics: the water heats to saturation over the bottom of the core (more of it when the inlet is more subcooled or the flow higher), then boils; the quality rises to the exit, and the drift-flux relation gives the void (about 39 % average and 68 % at the exit at rated). Kinetics, fuel, voids and flow run in 0.05 s substeps inside each step.

Recirculation: one pump on a variable-speed MG set drives one jet pump, standing for two loops and 20 jet pumps. The drive flow (4300 kg/s at rated, 1.6 MPa pump head) leaves the nozzles fast and entrains twice its own flow from the downcomer; the mixed 12,900 kg/s goes up through the core. The core flow is where the jet pump's pressure rise plus the natural circulation head (the downcomer's water column against the lighter two-phase core and chimney) balances the core resistance, which grows with the steam quality. With the pump off the natural circulation alone gives about a third of rated flow at power. The voids in the core and chimney are the voids inside the level, so swell depends on flow and power; steam flashed beyond what the core boils (during a depressurisation) adds to them and clears in 0.7 s. The level shown is the downcomer water level, where the instrument taps are: the voids inside the shroud push their volume of water out into the downcomer. The recorder also shows the collapsed level, water alone.

Heat to the coolant comes from the fuel: fission and decay heat, with no other gains or losses. Steam leaves through the main steam line and the safety/relief valves at saturated-steam enthalpy; feedwater enters as compressed liquid at the heater outlet temperature.

The feedwater train is a hotwell, one pump, one heater and the feed valve. The flow is where the pump's pressure (10 MPa at full speed, falling with flow and with the square of speed) equals the vessel pressure plus the drops in the heater, the piping and the valve, so the same valve opening passes more water when the vessel pressure falls. The heater stands for the whole heater train: a shell in which steam extracted from the turbine condenses on the feed tubes. The extraction pressure rises in proportion to the main steam flow (2.4 MPa at rated). The extraction valve's opening sets how much steam reaches the shell, and so the shell pressure; the feed leaves 3 K below the shell's saturation temperature, 216 °C at rated with the valve open. With no steam to the turbine there is no heating, and the feed comes in at the hotwell temperature.

Assumptions and limits

  • Point kinetics: one node for the whole core, so no flux shape, no xenon, no local power peaking and no burnup. The feedback coefficients and β are round generic values for a beginning-of-cycle BWR, not from one plant. The rods are one lever with an S-shaped worth (critical near 60 % hot, about 80 % at rated; all in is about 21 dollars subcritical); real BWRs move rods one at a time in a sequence. If the reactivity reaches one dollar (prompt critical) the model stops. Scram is a button only: nothing trips it automatically yet (real plants scram on valve closure, high pressure, high power, low level); the rods go fully in over 3 s, a placeholder for the control rod drive hydraulics.
  • Core thermal-hydraulics: flat axial power, one average channel, no bypass flow, homogeneous two-phase pressure drop, illustrative jet pump and loss coefficients chosen to give the rated point. The power response to inlet subcooling (feedwater temperature) is stronger than in a real core. The void feedback loop through the 5 s fuel lag can oscillate at high power and low flow, as real BWRs can (density-wave and core-wide instability), but this lumped model is not a stability analysis. No recirculation pump heat (a few MW in a real plant), no pump suction or discharge valves, no seal or cavitation limits; the stopped pump passes some forward flow.
  • The downcomer is one well-mixed node of fixed volume: no stratification, no transport delay beyond its mixing time, and the feed does not condense any steam directly at the sparger.
  • The steam is always saturated, so there is no superheat in the dome. The dome holds no air or nitrogen: below 100 °C the pressure would fall below atmospheric, where a real vessel would have its head vent open.
  • Level is the volume of water and voids over a uniform cross-section of 28.6 m², from the inside bottom of the vessel: top of active fuel 9.3 m, normal level 14.3 m, main steam nozzle 16.5 m. The level is read in the downcomer, as real vessels do (against a reference leg); the model takes the downcomer and shroud regions as one free surface. The model stops if the level falls to the top of the fuel or reaches the steam nozzle.
  • The main steam valve stands for the main steam isolation valves together with the turbine control and bypass valves: 2000 kg/s fully open at 7.0 MPa, in proportion to pressure (choked), into the condenser. It strokes at most 20 % per second. On the mimic it sits at the turbine control valve.
  • Turbine stub: there is no turbine model yet. The steam expands along one fixed line from the throttle to the condenser pressure with an isentropic efficiency of 0.85, the heater's extraction steam leaving on the way, and the gross output is that work times 0.985. No moisture separator reheater, no shaft speed or stage pressures. With one heater stage drawing 46 % of the steam at 2.4 MPa the output is about 920 MWe at rated, lower than the 1100 MWe of a real plant with several heater stages and reheat.
  • Condenser: one steam space of 4000 m³ over the hotwell, saturated at its own temperature. The exhaust and the heater drains flash or condense into it, and the tube bundle condenses steam at a rate set by the circulating water: 38 m³/s at 20 °C, fixed for now, through a bundle sized (not sourced) for about 7 kPa at rated. The condensate falls into the hotwell at the shell temperature, so the hotwell and the feed pump suction follow the load: 39 °C at rated, colder at low load. No air in the shell yet. Above 0.135 MPa the LP turbine exhaust diaphragms would blow, and the model stops.
  • Safety/relief valves: eleven, each with a spring set pressure and a rated capacity from a GE BWR/4 training lesson plan: four at 1090 psig (7.62 MPa abs) rated 800,000 lb/h (101 kg/s), four at 1100 psig (7.69 MPa) 808,000 lb/h, three at 1110 psig (7.75 MPa) 815,000 lb/h. Hover a valve's button for its opening pressure and capacity. A spring opens its valve fully above the set pressure and recloses it 0.25 MPa lower; that blowdown is an illustrative figure, not from the source. Flow scales in proportion to pressure from the rated capacity, into a suppression pool at 0.1 MPa that does not heat up. Opening by hand stands for the valve's relief (pneumatic) mode; there is no automatic relief logic, no low-low set and no automatic depressurisation system.
  • Feedwater: the hotwell is the water under the condenser; it changes with the condensate, makeup, feed, and steam lost through the safety/relief valves. It holds 600 t; makeup from the condensate storage tank, or reject to it, is up to 100 kg/s. The condensate, booster and feed pumps are one pump with an illustrative curve (10 MPa shutoff at full speed), no trips, no minimum-flow line and no startup valve; below 5 % hotwell it loses suction gradually. One heater stage stands for the train, with one throttling valve on its extraction line; real plants have several low- and high-pressure stages whose extraction lines carry only isolation and non-return valves. The outlet follows with a 60 s lag. The heater drains go to the condenser, which flashes them. The cleanup (reject) flow is not modelled, so there is no way to drain water during heat-up other than the valves.
  • Plant layout: the mimic draws the whole plant as a draft. Solid parts are modelled; dashed grey parts (containment, MSIVs, stop and bypass valves, moisture separator reheater, circulating water, air ejector and its motive steam, vacuum pump, condensate pump, polisher, LP heater) are drawn only and do nothing yet. The main steam valve is drawn as the turbine control valve; the heater's extraction is drawn from the HP turbine. The HP and LP turbines and the generator are drawn solid for the turbine stub, the recirculation pump, its loop and the jet pump for the recirculation model.
  • No vessel or internals heat capacity: the steel would slow heat-up and pressure changes. Generic numbers for a large BWR (about 3300 MW thermal); not any specific plant. All pressures are absolute.
