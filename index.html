Relief valve

Recloses 2 % below (at least 0.2 MPa). Set it low, about 3.6 MPa, while the coolant is cold. The safety valve at 17.2 MPa is always armed.

Running a startup

  1. Click Cold, water-solid. Every control starts manual. The pressuriser is full of liquid at 2.8 MPa, and liquid barely compresses, so a small mismatch between charging and letdown, or any heating, moves the pressure fast. Trim letdown by hand, or pick Letdown holds the pressure setpoint under Automatic assists. Then turn the heaters up; at 1.8 MW the pressuriser warms about 30 °C per hour.
  2. At about 230 °C the pressuriser reaches saturation and a steam bubble forms: the level gauge changes from Solid to a number. Nothing switches over for you. Letdown can no longer control pressure, so move charging and letdown to level control, or drain the level by hand with letdown above charging, and hold pressure with the heaters, by hand or with the heater and spray assist.
  3. Heat the coolant with a positive net heat and raise the pressure step by step, keeping the subcooling margin positive. Raise the relief opening pressure ahead of the operating pressure; plants disarm low-temperature relief above about 150 °C.
  4. For shutdown, start from Cool down from hot standby, which sets the net heat to −13.5 MW. The shrinking coolant draws water out of the pressuriser, so charge to hold the level and lower the pressure as the coolant cools.
  5. Below about 175 °C and near 2.8 MPa, lower the relief opening pressure to about 3.6 MPa and fill the pressuriser: level setpoint 100 %, or charging above letdown. The cooler water collapses the bubble. Once it is solid, turn the heaters off and put letdown back on pressure control, or the pressure climbs to the relief setpoint.

What the model solves

The pressuriser (51 m³) tracks its own water mass and energy. When the liquid fills the vessel it is treated as compressed liquid; otherwise it is a saturated mixture. The coolant system (280 m³) is one block of liquid at one temperature. A single net-heat control stands for pump work and core or decay heat minus RHR or steam dump; the pumps are assumed to run, keeping the coolant mixed and the spray supplied. Its heat capacity includes 0.6 GJ/K of steel.

Each step the surge flow is solved so the pressuriser and the coolant system are at the same pressure. Charging and letdown change the coolant mass, and spray takes cold-leg water into the steam space.

Assumptions and limits

  • Insurge water collects as a cooler layer at the bottom and mixes into the saturated water over about 5 minutes. The layer compresses the steam, which is why pressure rises on an insurge. Otherwise liquid and steam are in equilibrium, so the brief pressure dip before flashing on an outsurge is not shown.
  • Charging enters at coolant temperature, so its cooling effect is ignored.
  • Liquid compressibility uses approximate saturated-liquid values, about 0.44 per GPa at 50 °C rising to 3 per GPa at 340 °C.
  • Relief valve flow scales with pressure: steam about 15 kg/s at 16.2 MPa, falling in proportion; water about 30 kg/s at 3.6 MPa, rising with the square root of pressure. The safety valve passes four times as much. These are rough sizing figures, not a valve model.
  • Heaters cut out below 12 % level to protect the elements; this interlock is always on.
  • The model stops if the coolant reaches saturation, the pressuriser dries out, or the pressure nears the critical point. Values are typical of a 4-loop PWR, not of any specific plant.
