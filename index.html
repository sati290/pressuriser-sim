Automatic control

Charging, letdown, heaters, spray and the relief valve each have an M/A switch. In A the slider follows the automatic demand and is greyed out. In M the slider is yours and a notch marks the demand, so you can match it before switching to A. The settings here choose the logic behind the demands. Nothing switches between M and A, or between logics, by itself.

Heaters and spray

Pressure setpoint: heaters run at 20 % at the setpoint and full power 0.12 MPa below it; spray starts 0.1 MPa above and is full 0.5 MPa above. ΔT: the pressuriser is held that much hotter than the coolant, so with a bubble the pressure target is the saturation pressure at coolant + ΔT and falls as the coolant cools; heaters are full 1 K short, spray starts 0.5 K over and is full 3 K over. The default 53 K gives 15.5 MPa at the 291.7 °C hot-standby coolant temperature. Water-solid, the heaters still raise pressure but spray does little. Letdown pressure control always uses the pressure setpoint.

With a bubble, the ΔT is also the coolant's subcooling margin, since the pressuriser sits at saturation. Over a cold coolant it means little pressure (60 °C + 53 K is 0.16 MPa); real plants also keep a minimum pressure for the coolant pump seals, which is not modelled. Plants limit this ΔT (spray thermal stress, about 180 K); the stats flag it above 180 K.

Charging and letdown

The named flow does the controlling. The other one, if in A, holds the flow it had when switched to A and moves off it only while the first is at 0 or 20 kg/s.

Relief valve

In A the valve opens fully above this pressure and recloses 2 % below (at least 0.2 MPa). Set it low, about 3.6 MPa, while the coolant is cold. The safety valve at 17.2 MPa is always armed.

Running a startup

  1. Click Cold, water-solid. Every control starts manual. The pressuriser is full of liquid at 2.8 MPa, and liquid barely compresses, so a small mismatch between charging and letdown, or any heating, moves the pressure fast. Trim letdown by hand, or switch letdown to A with Letdown holds the pressure setpoint selected under Automatic control. Then turn the heaters up; at 1.8 MW the pressuriser warms about 30 °C per hour.
  2. At about 230 °C the pressuriser reaches saturation and a steam bubble forms: the level gauge changes from Solid to a number. Nothing switches over for you. Letdown can no longer control pressure, so select Charging holds the level setpoint and switch charging and letdown to A, or drain the level by hand with letdown above charging. Hold pressure with the heaters, by hand or with heaters and spray in A.
  3. Heat the coolant with a positive net heat and raise the pressure step by step, keeping the subcooling margin positive. Raise the relief opening pressure ahead of the operating pressure; plants disarm low-temperature relief above about 150 °C.
  4. For shutdown, start from Cool down from hot standby, which sets the net heat to −13.5 MW. The shrinking coolant draws water out of the pressuriser, so charge to hold the level and lower the pressure as the coolant cools, by hand or with heaters and spray in A holding the pressuriser − coolant ΔT.
  5. Below about 175 °C and near 2.8 MPa, lower the relief opening pressure to about 3.6 MPa and fill the pressuriser: level setpoint 100 %, or charging above letdown. The cooler water collapses the bubble. Once it is solid, turn the heaters off and select letdown pressure control again, or the pressure climbs to the relief setpoint.

What the model solves

The pressuriser (51 m³) tracks its own water mass and energy. When the liquid fills the vessel it is treated as compressed liquid; otherwise it is a saturated mixture. The coolant system (280 m³) is one block of liquid at one temperature. A single net-heat control stands for pump work and core or decay heat minus RHR or steam dump; the pumps are assumed to run, keeping the coolant mixed and the spray supplied. Its heat capacity includes 0.6 GJ/K of steel.

Each step the surge flow is solved so the pressuriser and the coolant system are at the same pressure. Charging and letdown change the coolant mass, and spray takes cold-leg water into the steam space.

Assumptions and limits

  • Insurge water collects as a cooler layer at the bottom and mixes into the saturated water over about 5 minutes. The layer compresses the steam, which is why pressure rises on an insurge. Otherwise liquid and steam are in equilibrium, so the brief pressure dip before flashing on an outsurge is not shown.
  • Charging enters at coolant temperature, so its cooling effect is ignored.
  • Liquid compressibility uses approximate saturated-liquid values, about 0.44 per GPa at 50 °C rising to 3 per GPa at 340 °C.
  • Relief valve flow scales with pressure: steam about 15 kg/s at 16.2 MPa, falling in proportion; water about 30 kg/s at 3.6 MPa, rising with the square root of pressure. The safety valve passes four times as much. These are rough sizing figures, not a valve model.
  • Heaters cut out below 12 % level to protect the elements; this interlock is always on.
  • The model stops if the coolant reaches saturation, the pressuriser dries out, or the pressure nears the critical point. Values are typical of a 4-loop PWR, not of any specific plant.
